# Prehospital Critical Care to Nursing Assessment Bridging

## Purpose and Scope
This document provides a structured public framework for translating rapid, field-oriented clinical pattern recognition into systematic nursing assessment, prioritization, and care-planning reasoning.

All examples in this document are synthetic and de-identified. This file contains educational frameworks only and does not provide patient-specific medical advice, clinical orders, or local EMS/hospital protocols.

## Cognitive Shift: Field Triage to Structured Nursing Reasoning
Prehospital care often emphasizes immediate stabilization, scene management, and rapid transport under dynamic constraints. Inpatient and academic nursing assessment expands that foundation into ongoing trend evaluation, structured prioritization frameworks, interdisciplinary coordination, and patient/family education.

| Dimension | Prehospital / Critical Transport Lens | Academic & Inpatient Nursing Lens | Bridging Focus |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Primary Tempo | Rapid initial stabilization and transport readiness | Continuous surveillance, trend synthesis, and care progression | Knowing when rapid stabilization shifts into longitudinal monitoring |
| Information Horizon | Immediate scene/transport window and handoff snapshot | Shift-to-shift trajectory, lab/diagnostic trends, and discharge readiness | Connecting initial physiological response to downstream complications |
| Decision Framing | Protocol-guided actions and time-critical escalations | Clinical judgment, safety prioritization, and nursing interventions | Mapping field actions to nursing rationales, contraindications, and escalation criteria |

## Ontology Mapping Template
Use the nine-part nursing ontology to organize synthetic study cases cleanly across domains:

1. **Physiology:** Underlying mechanisms driving the clinical presentation.
2. **Assessment:** Focused subjective and objective data collection across body systems.
3. **Findings:** Expected versus concerning cues, vital sign patterns, and diagnostic indicators.
4. **Differential:** Competing conditions or causes to evaluate systematically.
5. **Interventions:** Safe, evidence-aligned nursing and collaborative actions.
6. **Contraindications:** Conditions, precautions, or risk factors that alter or preclude an intervention.
7. **Monitoring:** Ongoing parameters, reassessment intervals, and response markers.
8. **Complications:** Anticipated adverse events or decompensation pathways.
9. **Escalation:** Clear thresholds and communication steps for provider notification or rapid response.

## Synthetic Study Example: Respiratory Deterioration Pattern
* **Context (Synthetic):** An adult patient presents with progressive dyspnea, tachypnea, and accessory muscle use after an acute respiratory insult.
* **Physiology:** Impaired ventilation-perfusion matching and increased work of breathing elevate oxygen demand and risk fatigue-related ventilatory failure.
* **Assessment:** Evaluate airway patency, respiratory rate and rhythm, work of breathing, lung sounds, mental status, pulse oximetry, and perfusion.
* **Findings:** Tachypnea, intercostal retractions, diminished or adventitious breath sounds, anxiety or restlessness, and falling oxygenation saturation.
* **Differential:** Acute bronchospasm, pulmonary edema, pneumonia/sepsis-related respiratory compromise, or impending ventilatory fatigue.
* **Interventions:** Position for optimal chest expansion (such as upright/high-Fowler's as tolerated), administer supplemental oxygen per order/protocol, prepare suction and airway support if indicated, and provide calm reassurance.
* **Contraindications:** Avoid lying flat when dyspnea is acute unless clinically required for a specific procedure; verify medication and oxygen delivery limits against patient status and orders.
* **Monitoring:** Track respiratory rate, effort, breath sounds, oxygenation trends, blood pressure/heart rate response, and mental status at frequent intervals.
* **Complications:** Hypoxemic or hypercapnic respiratory failure, exhaustion/apnea, or hemodynamic instability.
* **Escalation:** Escalate immediately to the covering clinician or rapid response team if work of breathing worsens, mental status declines, oxygenation fails to improve despite support, or impending fatigue is observed.
